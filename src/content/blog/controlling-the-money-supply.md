Every time the government spends a dollar, a new dollar is created and enters the money circulation. Every time the government collects a dollar in taxes, a dollar leaves the money circulation.

We can alternately say that taxes pay for government spending but that was true during the “gold is money” days. Back then, the government could not just create gold so they needed to collect gold as taxes in order to pay for government spending. Today, a dollar is backed by nothing and the government can create as many dollars as it wants. So in theory, the government does not need to collect taxes to pay for it’s spending.

But taxation is important. If the government kept on spending money but did not collect any taxes, eventually too much money would end up in the system. That would mean extreme inflation. Taxation helps the government keep the money supply at appropriate levels.

By tweaking the levels of taxation and government spending, the government can control the supply of money. When the economy is overheating, the government should tax more and spend less, thereby decreasing the supply of money. During a recession, the government should tax less and spend more, thereby increasing the supply of money in the system.

*That’s a pretty simple concept. So it’s very odd that we see politicians calling for austerity measures during a recession and increased spending during the boom times. Exactly the wrong things to do!*

Using taxes and government spending as the only levers in controlling the money supply has one major problem though. They’re political. And cannot be changed frequently. So they cannot be used to react quickly to changing economic conditions. Solution: interest rates.

Every time you lend money to the government by buying treasuries, you’re temporarily taking these dollars out of circulation. Every time the government pays back the bond with interest, more money than before enters the money circulation. By changing the interest rates, the government can temporarily control the money supply. But while taxation destroys the dollar permanently, increasing interest rates only destroys the dollar temporarily and will actually lead to even more money supply in the future.
